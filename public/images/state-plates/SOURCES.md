# Design references

Selected September 5, 2026. Variant choices are subjective. References identify the real plates that inspired the redraws; no reference photographs are embedded or bundled. Detailed scenic artwork, typefaces, silhouettes, and small symbols have been simplified, and decorative elements are sometimes moved or lightened to accommodate text. This is not a claim of exact fidelity or current availability.

The general visual survey was [Rick Kretschmer’s plate gallery](https://www.ricksplates.com/uscurrent.htm). Historic variants were checked against the linked state histories. Supplemental references include [Alabama’s Gulf Coast design](https://www.wbrc.com/2022/01/07/alabama-beaches-take-center-stage-states-new-license-plates/), [Alaska’s artistic plate](https://www.plateshack.com/y2k/Alaska3/ak3y2k.html), and [South Dakota plate history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_South_Dakota).

## Adaptation notes

* California and Michigan use retro colorways. Nebraska, New York and other states deliberately use older designs.
* State names remain, but official fonts have been approximated with outlined lettering.
* County names, plate numbers and expiration stickers are omitted everywhere.
* Some central motifs are reduced in contrast to improve dynamic number visibility.
* Source photographs remain the property of their respective owners. The bundle contains original vector drawing code and illustrations, not the reference photos.
